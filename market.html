From Common Basis to Two-Sided Market.

A proposed brokerage and market-data business built around standardized AI compute procurement.

Concept-stage market. This page describes a proposed business and potential market-development path. Standard Basis does not represent that it currently operates a marketplace, publishes a benchmark, or offers forward or derivative contracts.

The Business

Standard Basis is the company. CCBT is the denomination. The marketplace is the initial product. Brokerage is the initial revenue model. Market data is the emerging moat.

CCBT by itself is a standard; Standard Basis is proposed as the market around it. On one side are AI companies and enterprises seeking inference capacity. On the other are neoclouds, hyperscalers, hosting providers, and other owners of qualifying compute capacity.

The proposed initial service is legible without requiring every customer to begin with the methodology: describe the workload and commercial requirements; Standard Basis normalizes the request, solicits competing quotes, compares the economics on a common basis, and helps structure and execute the transaction.

Heterogeneous offers need not become identical. CCBT is proposed as the common reference; real service attributes and frictions remain visible as premiums, discounts, and contract terms around that basis.

The Brokerage Wedge

Day-one value would come from solving an ordinary commercial problem: find buyers, find sellers, standardize the request, obtain competitive prices, and complete a transaction. A broker can begin with imperfect public reference data because the act of brokering generates better private market evidence.

Ppublic → Pquoted → Ptransacted

Potential revenue layers, each dependent on real demand and appropriate permissions, could include transaction commissions, procurement software, contract administration, market-data subscriptions, and benchmark or index licensing. Risk-taking, financing, or regulated financial products would be later and materially different businesses.

Market Data and Benchmark Flywheel

Every standardized RFQ provides evidence about demand. Every provider quote provides evidence about supply. Every completed transaction provides an observed price at which the two meet.

CounterpartiesQualified buyers and sellers
Market activityRequests, quotes, transactions

Market utilityEasier comparison and contracting

This sequencing matters: the marketplace and benchmark can reinforce one another. It also creates conflicts and governance obligations. A credible benchmark cannot simply be a marketing output of a broker; methodology, input controls, oversight, transparency, manipulation resistance, and auditability would need deliberate design.

Buy the AI. Choose the Model Later.

A buyer may know the quantity of future AI it expects to consume without knowing the future model. A proposed deliverable CCBT structure would let the buyer fix a quantity and price basis today while retaining model-selection flexibility at delivery.

How much AI? — decide today
What price basis? — agree today
Which qualifying model? — choose later

The basket defines the denomination, not the buyer’s consumption mix. At delivery, a contracted CCBT purchasing balance could be allocated among qualifying models according to then-prevailing conversion prices and the specific delivery agreement.

This promise is not automatic. A provider would need credible delivery rights, substitution rules, service specifications, credit support, and remedies. The contract — not the index formula alone — determines what has been sold.

A Long-Term Development Path

Brokerage

Match compute demand and supply.

Transaction data

Observe market prices and basis.

Benchmarks

Develop credible spot and forward references.

Contracts

Standardize commercial structures.

Liquidity

Explore secondary and risk markets.

The progression is an ambition, not a forecast. Spot procurement does not automatically produce a derivatives market. Each stage requires sufficient participation, repeatable transactions, credible data, legal analysis, risk management, and market trust.

What Must Be True

  • DemandBuyers must value cross-provider comparison, flexible procurement, or future model choice.
  • SupplyProviders must be willing and able to quote standardized requests and make credible delivery commitments.
  • DataMarket evidence must be representative enough to support useful methodology without overstating precision.
  • ContractsService quality, basis, substitution, settlement, credit, and default must be explicit.
  • GovernanceBrokerage incentives and benchmark administration require robust separation and oversight.
  • RegulationForward, derivative, benchmark, brokerage, and marketplace activities require jurisdiction-specific legal analysis.

The credible path starts with helping one buyer obtain a better, more comparable set of compute offers — then doing it repeatedly.
